# Study plans, daily schedules, and weekly goals

These plans assume you can already build and test a full-stack application. Start from the [19-module roadmap](index.md). Module numbers below refer to that route. Use the [portfolio briefs](portfolio.md) for project scope and acceptance evidence.

## Choose a pace

| Plan | Weekly study budget | Approximate total | Intended outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 months / 26 weeks | 20–25 hours | 520–650 hours | Strong application foundations, broad awareness, and one engineered capstone. |
| 12 months / 52 weeks | 12–15 hours | 624–780 hours | Deeper model understanding, several evaluated projects, and a production/research introduction. |
| 24 months / 104 weeks | 10–12 hours | 1,040–1,248 hours | Shared foundations plus sustained specialization, operational experience, and independent research practice. |

The six-month path is intensive, not a shortcut to mastery of every advanced field. The longer plans create more room for revisiting mathematics and running independent experiments. Calendar progress does not override the exit gates.

If you miss a gate, use the next review week or extend the plan. Do not compress debugging and evaluation to preserve an arbitrary deadline. If your Python or mathematics diagnostic exposes gaps, add two to six foundation weeks and shift later milestones.

## Daily schedule

### A two-hour weekday session

| Time | Activity | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 15 minutes | Recall yesterday's idea without notes | A short explanation or calculation. |
| 30 minutes | Read one focused section or watch part of a lecture | One question and a concise note. |
| 60 minutes | Implement, modify, or test an example | A runnable change and observed result. |
| 15 minutes | Record errors and plan the next experiment | A learning-log entry and next action. |

For the **12-month plan**, do five two-hour sessions plus a two- to five-hour weekend project block: 12–15 hours. Keep one day free when possible.

For the **6-month plan**, do five three-hour sessions plus five to ten weekend hours: 20–25 hours. Add implementation and evaluation time rather than only more videos. Include breaks and split long blocks.

For the **24-month plan**, do five 90-minute sessions plus a 2.5- to 4.5-hour project/review block: 10–12 hours. Put one substantial experiment or review before adding optional resources.

## Weekly rhythm and goals

- **Monday:** state one learning objective, prerequisites, and an observable acceptance criterion.
- **Tuesday:** work a small example by hand, then reproduce it in code.
- **Wednesday:** change an input or assumption and predict the result.
- **Thursday:** integrate the idea into the current project and test failures.
- **Friday:** explain the result from memory and answer three interview questions.
- **Weekend block:** run the planned comparison, clean up the project, and write a one-page decision/report.
- **Weekly review:** keep one concept explanation, one runnable artifact, one failed assumption, and one next step.

Use active recall and spaced review: revisit the idea the next day, the next week, and roughly a month later. Pair interview preparation with implementation; a memorized definition without an example is not the goal.

## Twenty-four-month plan: 104 weeks

Use eight 13-week quarters. Within each quarter, the first month has 4 weeks, the second 4 weeks, and the third 5 weeks. Thus the 24 month rows below account for all 104 weeks.

For a **four-week month**: week 1 study and trace; week 2 implement a baseline; week 3 run a controlled comparison; week 4 evaluate, explain, and package. For a **five-week month**, week 5 is an independent review, repair, or replication week. Apply that rhythm to the concrete deliverable in each row.

The final quarter is where you deepen one field. For robotics, add the control and mechanics prerequisites before learned policies. For research on model training, add stronger optimization and distributed-training work. For architecture, spend the same time on measured operational experience and design reviews.

## Monthly self-assessment

Answer these without relying on a tutorial:

1. Can I explain the idea and work a small example?
2. Can I implement or modify it and verify the result?
3. Can I name a simpler alternative and compare it fairly?
4. Can I diagnose at least one failure?
5. Can another developer reproduce my evidence?

If an answer is no, choose a repair task before adding a new framework. Use the existing [checkpoints](../assessments.md) and the module interview questions as practice, then demonstrate the skill in a project.
